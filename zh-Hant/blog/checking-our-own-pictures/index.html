Checking Our Own Pictures Against the Past

2026-11-14 14:15 KST

Iltonggi is a game, so some things are adapted on purpose. But there is a difference between adapting something and getting it wrong without noticing. Recently we sat down and compared our own pictures with what murals and excavated objects show. This post is a note on what we found, including the parts that did not match. The game is still in development, and the work is still going on.

1. How we checked

The standard was modest. We did not ask whether every detail is perfect. We asked whether the silhouette, the shape you notice at a glance, agrees with what murals and excavated objects show. For each item we wrote down one of three words: matches, partly, or off. Where we were not sure of a fact, we marked it as unverified and did not state it.

2. A Gaya king in a Silla crown

The portrait of Gaya's last king wore a crown with three upright pieces shaped like the character for mountain. That is the look of Silla's gold crowns, whose uprights are shaped like the character for "out" and like antlers. Crowns linked to Daegaya, a Gaya state, are known for uprights shaped like plants and flowers. So we had put a Silla-style crown on a Gaya king. We did not find enough to say whether a gold crown existed for the other Gaya state, so we are not claiming one.

3. A collar that arrived too late

The same portrait had a round collar. In the records, Silla adopted that Chinese-style round collar in the year 649. Gaya had already fallen by 562. The old murals show the straight, y-shaped collar. A collar that came nearly a century later is a small thing, but a king's clothes are the first thing a face is read through.

4. A Baekje ship with the wrong sail

Our Baekje ship had a sail with tight horizontal ribs. That reads as a Chinese junk. Direct evidence for sail structure in that period is thin, and we are leaving that part unverified. What the ship-shaped pottery of Gaya and Silla does show is a long hull with a raised bow and stern. The hull was fine. The sail was the problem.

5. Roofs that curve too much

On one fortress scene, the eaves curved up sharply. That looks like Joseon or Chinese architecture. Only a few traces of gate towers from the Three Kingdoms period remain, so the true roof shape is unverified. What we can say is that a roof with strong curves was the wrong direction. A Japanese-style tower appears only in Act 2, where it was meant to mark the difference.

Finally

We are not saying every item is fixed. We are saying we wrote down where we were off, and we are going through them in order of how visible they are. Where we adapted on purpose, such as keeping some people alive past their real deaths in Acts 8 and 9, we tag it as an adaptation instead of hiding it. A game can bend history. It should not do it silently.
