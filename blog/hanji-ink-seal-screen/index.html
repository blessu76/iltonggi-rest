Hanji, Ink and the Seal: Choosing a Face for the Map

2026-11-03 21:05 KST

The map is the thing players look at longest in Iltonggi. The twenty-four towns sit in the same places whatever we do, but the feel of the screen changes a great deal depending on what kind of paper the land is drawn on and how. According to our development records, we made three look drafts for the map and compared them: a tomb-mural style, an ink wash with light colour, and an old-map style. This post records which materials we chose for each, and what we decided not to change in any of them. The screens are as they stood in development, and the final look may differ.

One: the tomb mural, red outlines on a clay wall

The first draft follows the impression of Goguryeo tomb murals. We repainted the land's brightness along a colour ramp that runs from dark brown to pale ochre, and split the brightness into only five steps so that it reads like flat colouring. Borders are thick, even lines in a dark reddish brown. Places the player has not yet visited are hidden behind a clay-coloured plaster wall with a cloud-scroll pattern. Name labels use a dark reddish-brown ground with light lettering, meant to look like an inscription on a wall.

Two: ink wash, hanji margins and brush lines

The second draft is the most stripped back. The land is painted in a greyscale ramp from ink black to hanji white, and each kingdom's territory is laid over it as a thin multiplied wash, as if the pigment had bled. The border is a brush line whose thickness wavers slightly from point to point. A line drawn straight at one thickness looks like an engineering drawing, so we let the width wobble along the segments.

The fog is where we spent the most care. Instead of painting over places not yet visited, we leave them as blank hanji margin and let ink seep in only along the edges. Name labels have no background, only ink lettering with a paper-coloured glow around the glyphs so they stay readable on the land. Troop counts appear as a square seal in the kingdom's colour. The choice was to make the number look stamped rather than boxed.

Three: the old map, mountain ridges and waterways

The third draft was made with the old maps of late Joseon, the Daedongyeojido among them, in mind. The land is the yellowed colour of paper, and on mountain tiles a ridge line is drawn in ink, joining three jagged peaks. Rivers and coasts are emphasised with a blue ink line, and the sea side gets two wavy lines. Borders are even lines with a thin dotted ink line down the middle, like a survey line. The fog is fine hatching, meant to say "land not yet drawn on the map".

What we did not change in any of the three

The three drafts share some rules. First, the four kingdom colours stay exactly the same in every draft. Only the texture and bleed of the lines change. If Goguryeo's red and Baekje's blue shifted from draft to draft, the distinctions players have learned would fall apart.

Second, roads keep the same thickness. During development the roads inside the recoloured ground got buried under the kingdom wash and were hard to see. We fixed this by drawing roads on top of the wash and the ridge lines as a double line, an outer and an inner stroke, and keeping the inner stroke always lighter than the land.

Third, at night each draft gets its own tinted cover and every town lights a lamp. The tomb-mural style sinks into a purplish dark, and the ink wash into a bluish grey one.

Closing

Change the paper, and the same twenty-four towns look like the land of a different country. We should say that these drafts borrow the impression of old paintings and maps, and are not copies of any particular work. We have also not checked what we say about the Daedongyeojido against an original.
