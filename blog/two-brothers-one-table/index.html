Two Brothers and One Table, Choosing a Side in Act 9

2026-11-11 12:00 KST

Act 9, "The Road Above the Clouds", is set in the Andes around the late 1520s, when two brothers each held a half of a great realm and could not agree. It is the first act where the ending depends on whom you befriended. This post explains how that works and why we built it. The act is still in development and may change.

1. Three nations on a vertical map

The map has 21 regions and three nations: Quito in the north, Cusco in the south, and your own United Kingdom, an invented kingdom of ours that is placed beside them. The mountains are squeezed into a tall, narrow map, and distances and directions are adapted to fit. We call the two brothers' realms by the names of their seats, Quito and Cusco, rather than by a larger label.

2. Two brothers who cannot ally

The two brother nations start with a bad relationship, and they cannot ally with each other at all. A proposal between them is refused, and the computer will not even consider it. That rule is the heart of the act. You can be friends with one, with the other, or with neither for a while, but you cannot gather them both under one agreement.

3. An ending that remembers who you stood beside

The first brother nation you ally with decides your side, and it stays your side even if the alliance breaks later. If you win without ever allying with either, the closing text is one of reconciliation: the two brothers sit at one table, and your kingdom promises not to lend its sword to a quarrel between them. If you win having stood with one, the other brother does not come to the table, and the line of that promise is left blank. We wanted the empty space to say something.

4. A goal that asks you to wait

The goal is to hold a town on the mountain road, an administrative town in the middle of the land (our reading of it is still unverified), to the end of 20 rounds, and if you lose it you start the count again. In earlier acts a single round was enough. Here the point is patience, which we thought suited a road rather than a battlefield.

5. What we left out

There are no sacrifices, sacred objects or sacred patterns on the soldiers or the map. The town where a famous capture took place is not used as a place name, and neither are the great lake and the sacred mountains. The people who crossed the sea in that era are simply absent from the map, so the act asks a different question from the usual story of that time. The two brothers both stay alive to the end of the game, and we mark that as an adaptation, since in history neither did.

Finally

Whether a choice between brothers can be offered without offending the descendants of either side is something we have written down as a question for the people closest to it. Several names and spellings are also still unverified. If we have misjudged, we would rather change it than defend it.
