A Three Kingdoms Land Grab for Your Phone

2026-10-30 12:00 KST

Land grab is a simple game. You draw lines in the dirt, flick a stone, and slowly carve out a little more territory, until the tension builds enough to flip the whole board. The rules take a few lines to explain, yet once you start, it's hard to put down. Iltonggi is our attempt to bring that fun onto a phone. It is set in the Three Kingdoms era, when Goguryeo, Baekje, Silla and Gaya stood against one another, and its name, Iltong (一統), means bringing the realm under a single banner. The 統 seal on the app icon comes from the same idea.

One round for a break

Our first goal was a game that gives a tired person an easy sense of accomplishment during a break. There are already plenty of strategy games that demand long stretches of time, so we decided to go the other way. A round is meant to finish in 5 to 10 minutes. The aim is to make autosave the default, so that closing the game at any moment costs you nothing, and if you leave for the menu mid-round, a message tells you "Saved."

So that newcomers don't feel pressured, the first game is a short 20 turns, and the enemy won't attack during the first two rounds. We wanted to give people time to get used to the rules first. We also set a principle of no devices that nag you to log in every day. When someone comes back after a while away, instead of rushing them, the game simply lets them know, briefly, that their game is still where they left it. We felt a game shouldn't hold a person's day in its grip. It should slip quietly into their breaks.

We also believe a sense of accomplishment doesn't come only from big victories. Winning a single town, or peeking at land hidden in fog thanks to a spy: the goal is for small moments like these to show up several times even within five minutes.

It starts with one town

The map works town by town. You tap a town to gather and train soldiers, then attack the neighbors. Bigger towns gather more troops, so the capital is large and the countryside small. That simple difference alone is enough to make you think about what to target first and what to defend.

Just as a game of janggi, Korean chess, ends when the king is captured, we kept the satisfaction of a game decided by planting a flag on the royal fortress. It isn't a game where you must swallow every last scrap of land. One decisive move can end it, which suits short rounds. Land you haven't seen is veiled in fog, and only a spy can peek at it. We felt a board where you press on, curious about the unknown, makes a better story than one where everything is visible at a glance.

We started with the smallest thing

In the first build we put on TestFlight, all you could do was choose a kingdom and a difficulty and play one round to the end. Weather, diplomacy and Direct Command came one by one after that. This blog is where we write that journey down in order: the struggle to tell four kingdoms apart by color, the worry of fitting people from fifteen hundred years ago into the right era, how we ran battles on a computer so Direct Command wouldn't become a cheat, and even the road that lets builds go out several times a day.

An empty spot on the shelf

Land-grab and conquest games are everywhere. But among the thirteen similar games we looked at, we didn't spot a short, single-player, turn-based game set in Korea's Three Kingdoms period. That gap is one of the reasons we started making this game. Of course, what we looked at isn't everything, so if we missed a game, the gap may be narrower than we think.

Iltonggi is not yet released and is being tested on TestFlight. We'll keep writing up what we run into as we build.
