A land grab can look simple in its rules, yet the shape of a game changes with where you strike early and where you hold back. This post gathers five things worth noticing first in your first game of Iltonggi. The values are from development and may change, and these are not the one right answer for every game, only a direction to start from.

## 1. The first game is short, and the first two rounds are safe

The first game is a short 20 turns. The first two rounds are safe, so during them you can get used to the screens and rules without worrying about being attacked. There is also a guided quest and a rules screen for first-time players, and it is worth a skim before you rush. Rather than spending the two safe rounds doing nothing, it is better to use them on gathering troops and scouting, as described below.

## 2. Troops grow only by recruiting

In Iltonggi troops do not grow by themselves each turn. Even in your capital, troops increase only when you recruit. The number you get in one go is higher when the population is larger, the morale is better and the town is more developed, so recruiting in a big town is more efficient.

Recruiting lowers the population by the same amount, and if morale is fine it slowly fills back. So drawing a little from several towns lasts longer than squeezing one town every turn. If you concentrate recruiting in the two safe rounds, you will have far more troops for your first fight.

## 3. Actions are limited

The number of things you can do in a turn is fixed, and the actions left show as 行 seal pips above the End Turn button. Moving troops one region costs one action, and moving two or three regions through your own land costs two. If only one action is left, long moves are blocked and you are told why.

So at the start of a turn it helps to decide the two or three things you must do. When sending troops, the 25%, 50%, 75% and 全 buttons under the slider are useful. The default is to send everything, but sending only part leaves troops to guard the towns behind.

## 4. Scout, and join hands if you need to

An enemy capital hidden in the fog only shows once you reveal it by scouting or a spy. The spy screen has a Scouting Suggestion that tells you where to look, so if you are not sure where, follow it first. Striking after one look is generally safer than striking without knowing the enemy's strength.

In diplomacy you can build goodwill by sending tribute to a neighbor, and an alliance takes about two tributes. There is also a pincer attack: if an ally attacked the same kingdom in the last round, that kingdom's defense bonus is lowered. Fighting several kingdoms at once with no alliance gets hard, so having at least one partner early lightens the load.

## 5. Pull back when it is slipping

When a fight feels badly lost, you can retreat and save half your troops. Commanding in person helps a little if you read the battle well, but it will not turn around a badly losing fight, so pulling back when it is not working is also a move.

Easy difficulty is tuned so that any kingdom you pick wins about as often as the others, and after you lose more than two games in a row, the defeat screen shows one tactic that fits how that game went. If you lost, read that line first.

## Finally

What is written here is only a direction for the start, and every game is different. If anything felt different from this post when you played, that is a clue for refining the rules further.
