In the previous post we introduced the 551 Han River scenario, the year Baekje and Silla joined hands to go after the Han River valley held by Goguryeo. Iltonggi's Han River scenario covers only that much. In real history, though, the alliance did not last. Three years later, in 554, the two kingdoms clashed around Gwansanseong, and King Seong of Baekje was killed. The game has no separate scene for it, but it shows what the 551 alliance eventually cost, so we are writing it down.

## What the shared land did to the partners

The details differ from record to record, but it is said that in 551 Baekje took the lower Han River and Silla the upper reaches. This was not their first partnership. The Baekje-Silla alliance is said to date from 433, so by now it had lasted almost 120 years.

According to the Samguk Sagi, in the seventh month of 553 Silla seized Baekje's northeastern frontier and set up a province there called Sinju. The man who sat as its governor is the Kim Mu-ryeok of our earlier post. The same record says that in the tenth month of that year the Baekje king sent his daughter to Silla's king as a junior consort. Whether it was an attempt to hold the relationship together after losing the land cannot be told from the records alone. What is clear is that the balance of the alliance had already tipped.

## Gwansanseong, 554

The next year, 554, King Seong drew in forces from the Gaya states, Daegaya among them, and from Wa, and attacked Silla. Many scholars place Gwansanseong around today's Okcheon in North Chungcheong. The Silla annals of the Samguk Sagi say that Silla was pushed back at first, but Kim Mu-ryeok, governor of Sinju, led troops against them, and Do-do, a high official of Samnyeonsan county, killed the Baekje king. The Baekje annals say that King Seong, wishing to surprise Silla, led fifty infantry and cavalry himself through the night and was killed after running into an ambush. The two accounts agree on one point: the king had gone to the front with only a small escort.

The Silla annals add that in this battle four of Baekje's highest ministers and 29,600 soldiers were beheaded and not a single horse returned. That is the winner's record, and we shouldn't take the figure at face value. Still, it seems clear that for Baekje this was a battle in which a king and many commanders were lost at once.

Iltonggi's map also has a Silla town called Samnyeonsanseong, on the road that leads from beyond the Sobaek mountains toward the Han River. A small town name on the board is tied to a scene like this, and that is one of the pleasures of historical research.

## What it left behind

After King Seong's death, King Wideok succeeded him, and the two kingdoms stayed estranged for a long time. Silla came to hold almost the whole Han River valley and gained a route toward the Yellow Sea. Gaya, which had stood on Baekje's side at Gwansanseong, is said to have fallen to a Silla attack in 562. Once the alliance snapped, the balance of power across a whole region changed.

## Alliances in the game

Iltonggi's diplomacy has alliances too. You can ally with only one kingdom at a time, and an alliance has a term, so when the remaining turns run out you get a notice that it has ended. There is also a pincer attack: if your ally hit the same kingdom within the last full round, that kingdom's defensive bonus shrinks. The picture of Baekje and Silla eyeing the Han River together in the 551 scenario is close to this rule.

You can break an alliance yourself, but it costs you. Goodwill with your former ally drops sharply, goodwill with other kingdoms drops too, and a kingdom that has broken an alliance before finds others unwilling to partner with it. The game turns the idea that breaking a promise is not free into a price in this way. We are not trying to explain the inner hearts of real historical figures with this rule.
