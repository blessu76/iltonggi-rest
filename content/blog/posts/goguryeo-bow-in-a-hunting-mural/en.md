Some mistakes only show up when you look at a picture up close. In Iltonggi, the Goguryeo archer carried a bow that stretched almost the full height of the frame. From a distance it read as a tall Japanese bow, or a long English one. This post is about how we caught it and what we changed. The game is still in development.

## What the tomb walls show

Goguryeo tomb paintings are among the best records we have of how people of that time hunted and rode. In the hunting scenes of several tombs, the archers hold a short bow. Even when it is drawn, it reaches only about half the height of the body. The upper end curves outward, away from the archer. Chinese records of the Three Kingdoms period, written about the eastern peoples, also say that Goguryeo made good bows, the kind called maek bows.

A short, strongly curved bow is easy to shoot from horseback. A long straight one is not. That is why the shape matters beyond looks.

## What we had drawn

Our archer's bow was one long, plain stick. It ran from near the top of the picture almost to the bottom. It looked like a bow from another region or another era. We judged it clearly wrong, because the murals and the written records point to the shorter shape.

## What we changed

We shortened the bow to about three quarters of the picture's height, and bent the upper end outward. The archer's cap was already right. It has the pointed shape seen in the same tombs, so we left it alone.

## What we did not change

Other acts are drawn with the gear of their own nations, on purpose. A helmet or a boat from the second act that looks foreign is not a mismatch, because that act is meant to show a different land. We compare each picture only with the people it is supposed to show.

## Finally

A bow is a small picture, but it is one of the first things many players notice about a soldier. One shape that is wrong up close can make a whole army feel like the wrong one. We changed it. Before we call the check finished, we will also look at the new bow at phone size.
