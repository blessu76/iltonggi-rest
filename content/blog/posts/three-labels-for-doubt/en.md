Checking a picture against history invites two mistakes. One is to change everything that feels off, even with no evidence. The other is to leave everything alone because nothing is certain. To avoid both, we give every finding one of three labels. This post explains them, using our check of the first act, which is set in the 4th to 7th centuries. The game is still in development.

## What we checked

The check covered the first act's 47 image files, from photos, opening screens and menus, plus the portraits, soldiers, animated scenes, map flags and town buildings the game draws. We compared each item with murals, excavated objects and early written records.

## The three labels

**Sure** means that a relic or a reliable old text backs the finding, and we change the picture. A Baekje noble's silver crown is an example. Its two silver leaves spread apart like horns, while excavated crowns have one central stem with matching side buds. The shape was wrong, so we fixed it.

**Likely** means the finding is plausible, but the evidence is thin. We record it and do not change the picture yet. The roofs on our gate towers are an example. They had a Joseon-style sweep, so we softened them, but we did not claim to know the true roof of a Three Kingdoms gate.

**Unknown** means we cannot judge it from what we have. We mark it [unverified] and ask the person who makes the final call. Straw hats and rain capes for soldiers are an example. We found no evidence for that gear in the period, so we did not change it, and we did not claim it was right.

## Why three and not two

With only right and wrong, every doubt becomes a yes or a no. The third label keeps the honest answer in the record. "We do not know yet" is a finding too, and it tells the next person where to look.

## What the labels do not do

The labels are not a stamp of truth. Some Sure items rest on a single relic, and a later find could change the picture again. We also did not search for new sources for this check. The references came from earlier checks and general knowledge of the period, so page numbers are unverified.

## Finally

An art check is only as useful as its record. Writing one label per finding, and keeping the doubtful ones visible, lets us change things with confidence and leave the rest for later.
