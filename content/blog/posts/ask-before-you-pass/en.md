A land grab game is, at its simplest, a game of taking land. Act 10, "The Oldest Land", breaks that on purpose. It is set around the year 1800 on a southern continent, and some of its regions cannot be taken at all. This post explains the rule and the reasoning. The act is still in development and we are still reviewing it, so it may change.

## 1. Regions that belong to someone

Seven regions on the map belong to the people of the land, whom the game calls the landowners. They are neutral from beginning to end. You cannot station troops there, you cannot send a spy there, and the computer kingdoms never aim at them either. Even in a hundred games played by the computer alone, those regions stay in the hands of their owners to the very end.

## 2. How you pass: ask first

If a landowner region lies between you and your goal, there is only one way through. You bring gifts and ask. Gifts build trust, and when the trust is high enough the road opens and you may pass. You do not own the land afterwards, and the game ends with the land still belonging to its owners.

We turned asking and hospitality into a few numbers, because a game needs them, and we know that simplifying it that way can feel light. We have listed that as a question for people who know better.

## 3. No names that are not ours to use

The landowners are not drawn as a nation, a king or commanders, and they are not a side you can choose. We did not use the names of real peoples or real persons, and we did not use any place that is sacred. Places on the map are named by plain features such as the northern cape, the river mouth, the central plain and the southern forest. We also avoided the modern colonial place names.

## 4. What the story is, and what it is not

The story is cut down to a round-the-continent voyage around 1800 and a sea-cucumber trade in the northern waters. Two fleets build a fort in the southeast. The act does not draw the real colonization and the harm that came with it. The closing text does not describe a song or a ceremony either, and ends instead with a line that says what was heard will not be written down.

## 5. Why we think this is better than leaving it out

Leaving the whole subject out would have been easier. We chose to include it, carefully and in a smaller form, because a game about unifying the world that never meets the people who were already there seemed to us a worse silence. We do not know yet whether the choice is right. Whether showing the landowners only as untouchable land reads as caring, or as treating them as if they were not there, is the question we most want answered.

## Finally

There is no dedicated music for this act and no new instrument sound. For now we are using the shared ambient sound only. If a consultation changes what this act should be, then the rule will stay but the numbers and the words will change.
