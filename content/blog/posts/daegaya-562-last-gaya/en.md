In the previous post we covered Gwansanseong in 554. Among the Gaya forces that stood with Baekje in that battle was Daegaya. This post is about 562, eight years later, the year usually counted as the final scene of Gaya history. Of the four kingdoms you can choose in Iltonggi, Gaya is the one that vanished earliest in real history. Choosing Gaya in the game is, in a way, choosing to push back against that ending.

## Gaya was not a single kingdom

The standard account is that Gaya was a cluster of small states along the Nakdong River. At first Geumgwan Gaya, around today's Gimhae, was the center, and from the late fifth century Daegaya, around today's Goryeong, is thought to have grown strong. It is said that in 532 Geumgwan Gaya surrendered to Silla under King Guhyeong. The Kim Mu-ryeok of our earlier post was this king's son. Even after Geumgwan Gaya was absorbed into Silla, Gaya forces centered on Daegaya remained.

## 562, in Silla's record

The Silla annals of the Samguk Sagi say that in the autumn of 562, the twenty-third year of King Jinheung, Gaya rebelled, and the king ordered Isabu to put it down, with Sadaham as his deputy. The word "rebelled" is the winner's word, written from Silla's point of view. Almost nothing survives from the Gaya side, so it is hard to know what Gaya people called these events.

According to Sadaham's biography, he was a hwarang, a young Silla warrior, who asked to join the campaign at fifteen or sixteen. The king at first refused because he was too young, but his resolve was firm and he was made a deputy commander. In the story that has come down, Sadaham went ahead and raised a white flag at the city gate, the people inside were so startled that they did not know what to do, and when Isabu's troops arrived behind him, Gaya surrendered at once. He is said to have declined his reward several times. The biography also tells of a friend, Mugwallang, with whom he had sworn to die together, and says that when Mugwallang died of illness, Sadaham grieved and followed him within days.

## What came after

After 562, no independent power calling itself Gaya appears in the records. Silla extended its land west of the Nakdong River. But the people did not vanish. Kim Mu-ryeok, a descendant of King Guhyeong, became a Silla general, and his great-grandson is said to be Kim Yu-sin, famous in the unification wars. The name Gaya closed, but people from Gaya kept their story going inside Silla.

## Gaya in Iltonggi

In Iltonggi, Gaya is the green kingdom you can pick alongside Goguryeo, Baekje and Silla. During development, some kingdoms, Gaya and Baekje among them, were hard to win with, so on Easy we tuned each kingdom so that any choice can win about equally often.

The Gaya king's crown was redrawn like a Gaya gold crown, with buds and leaves. In the 551 scenario the Gaya king is written as "the king of Daegaya" instead of by name, because the name is hard to pin down. We do not want to fill a nameless seat as if we knew.

The dates and people in this post have not been checked line by line against the original text of the Samguk Sagi either. Because the records disagree in places, we will correct anything that proves wrong.
